Different Timing

Foster Youth information moves through multiple systems, so reports may reflect different points in time. As data is updated across systems, temporary differences between reports may occur. Before assuming a data issue, first consider when each report was generated.

Students may enter foster care before data is updated in all systems.

Child welfare information must move through multiple systems before it appears in statewide reporting. Newly eligible students may require local verification until shared data has been updated.

Students may exit foster care before reports refresh.

When a student exits foster care, local records and statewide reports may not update at the same time. Kids move faster than their data. Temporary differences are expected until the next refresh.

Students may transfer between schools or districts.

Enrollment changes can affect where a student appears in reports, which organization is responsible for reporting, and when updated information becomes available across systems.

★
KEY TAKEAWAY

Foster Youth data reflects a point in time—not always the current moment. Because local and state systems update on different schedules, temporary differences between reports are expected as student information moves through the reporting process.

Before assuming a data issue, consider when each report was generated and what point in time it represents. When current information is needed for operational decisions, local verification is often the best source for confirming a student's most up-to-date status.
